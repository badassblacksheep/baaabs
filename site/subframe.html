Where the estimate comes from

The auction does not provide empty weight or weight ratings. We are assuming a 34,200 lb GVWR, a 22,000 lb full-fluid bus with seats, and 2,000 lb removed: 20,000 lb stripped and 14,200 lb available. The 12,000–16,000 lb range is a planning judgment, not a measured range for this VIN.

For context, a different 2012 All American D3 RE in a federal test weighed approximately 20,384 lb unloaded with full fluids and its seats installed; its GVWR was about 33,000 lb. It carried 47 students plus a driver and is not our donor’s configuration. Period Blue Bird literature advertised ratings up to 36,200 lb; that maximum is not assumed for our bus.

One driver's-side post vs four posts in two transverse pairs

We are setting aside exterior stations and comparing two chassis-supported interior architectures. The single post is inspired by the reported BAAAHS arrangement, not a verified copy of its engineering. The four-post option has two front/rear stations, each with a driver/passenger pair. Both aim to keep the cabin open; subwoofers sit as low as practical on separate chassis-connected mounts.

A · One post

One upright halfway along the driver's chassis rail. A moment-resisting grillage/hub must carry long cantilevers and eccentric roof loads. One post alone provides no inherent independent rotational restraint.

B · Four posts / two transverse pairs

Two transverse pairs at approximately one-third and two-thirds of the deck length. Shorter cantilevers and a wider support footprint, at the cost of four cabin uprights and additional foundations.

Fixed modeled deck: 36 ft long × 8 ft wide (10.9728 m × 2.4384 m). Fixed comparison assumptions: 225 lb/person, 1.5× illustrative dancing multiplier, 4,000 lb provisional centered deck dead load, and a centered crowd. The only variable here is dancer count.

The dead load is treated as centered; subs and generator are excluded because they are assumed independently mounted low. The 1.5× multiplier is a sensitivity assumption, not an approved dancing factor.

Structural interpretation: For the one-post case, each half of the deck is modeled as a uniformly loaded cantilever; for four posts, the deck has two interior transverse support lines with end overhangs of L/3, assuming no other end supports. The tabulated bending moments are idealized longitudinal values for a single equivalent beam, not grillage stresses. Real distribution, transverse torsion, deflection, uplift, fatigue and chassis load paths require a 3D model and engineering review. A four-post layout is not automatically adequate, and a single-post layout is not proven feasible.
