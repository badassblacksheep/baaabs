01 / What we now know

Individual posts. A different frame.

You report three individual posts inside BAAAHS. That makes an interior support system worth investigating. Their precise positions, roof framing, attachments and capacity are unknown; these drawings are our own trials.

The shared brief stays fixed: 20 people at 225 lb, dancing while moving up to 5 mph; 206 panels; 102-inch maximum road width. Keep the current deck footprint and height. Event deck/frame, skin, projecting mounts and speakers remain removable.

First candidate to develop: four individual posts near a chassis rail.

This reduces the trial roof spans compared with three posts and may let us incorporate posts into furniture. It is a starting layout for engineering, not a selected structural solution. Four individual posts means four total, versus eight posts in the previous four-station concept.

04 / What the physical connection needs to do

A continuous path into the chassis.

The roof-depth constraint: only about 7.9 inches.

The modeled bus roof is 3.2004 m above ground; the fixed deck surface is 3.4004 m. That leaves 200 mm (7.9 inches) at the crown before subtracting flooring, clearance and connections. A deep under-deck truss cannot be assumed to fit. Trial roof framing must satisfy this envelope; diagram heights are exaggerated for clarity.

Above the roof

Deck → transverse beams and longitudinal frame → removable bolted interfaces → individual posts. Keep transfer framing above the existing roof where the fixed deck height permits. Bracing or moment-resisting joints must stabilize the frame in both directions.

Through the cabin

Posts pass through detailed roof and floor openings. Preserve body/chassis movement and weather sealing. Do not assume the floor sheet or existing roof bows can take the new loads. Interior trim must allow access to the joints.

Below the floor

Post base → designed transfer assembly → verified chassis attachments. Check concentrated bearing, bending, uplift and torsion in the rails and connections. A post directly above a rail still needs this design.

The sheep skin: lighter removable regional panel frames attach to the primary roof frame and designed lower mounts. Their wind and handling loads belong in the analysis. Speakers and the head need dedicated load paths.

Road mode: interior posts and their retained fittings stay within the existing exterior. All projecting event assemblies come off. Retained post tops need their own stability check with the removable roof assembly absent.

05 / Width benefit, with an honest status

No exterior posts to add side width.

Interior supports can avoid increasing the donor’s width if every permanent fitting stays within its existing exterior envelope. That resolves the geometric source of our exterior-post problem; it does not establish the donor’s measured width, registration eligibility or complete road compliance.

The existing exterior v5 model still fails at approximately 103.35 inches. The interior concept has no complete retained-hardware model or road-width audit yet. The 102-inch invariant remains unchanged.

06 / Turn the concept into a lighter design

Develop four.
Keep three in the comparison.

We can develop the load model and connection concepts now. Final positions, member selection and fabrication release depend on the donor measurements that are currently deferred.

  1. Reserve interior routesMap four candidate posts against furniture, exits and chassis access. Check whether a slightly offset aisle preserves the cabin; the default over-rail case overlaps the centered test band by about 1.1 inches.
  2. Design roof and foundations togetherCompare three versus four individual posts, load distribution, transverse restraint, vibration and removable joints under the same moving-dance-floor requirements.
  3. Count the complete weightInclude roof framing, floor, posts, bases, joints, bracing and panel/equipment mounts. Compare against the 4,466-lb current frame/floor subtotal on an equivalent basis.
  4. Reconcile and reviewVerify the donor, retained road envelope, axle loads and vehicle stability; then produce reviewed member schedules and assembly drawings.
