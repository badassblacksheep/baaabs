Dancing while moving. Keep the modeled 37.4 × 8.9 ft deck, its 11.16 ft surface height above ground, and removable event assemblies. The 4,500-lb people allowance is a design input; it is not an approved occupancy.

Open to change: support count, sections, floor construction, truss depth, bracing and connections. Road mode retains the bus and permanent supports with every fitting; the event deck/frame, panels, projecting arms and speakers come off.

What is deliberately unresolved?

Measurements · deferred

Actual body/rub-rail envelope; chassis dimensions and attachment access; scale weights and axle ratings; equipment weights and locations.

Engineering · still to establish

Loads from dancing, stops, turns, bumps and wind; floor vibration; transverse restraint; joints and chassis flexibility; vehicle stability. A speed limit does not define these loads.

No modification selected

No rub-rail cutting, body welding, assumed roof-body capacity or final tube sizes. The deck footprint and height remain the comparison constraints.

A longer span has a cost.

The four-station trial increases the longest span from 10.6 to 12.8 ft. An unchanged simply supported beam under the same uniform load would see about 45% more bending demand and 2.11× deflection. That is a beam sensitivity—not a calculated truss response.

Try the same floor-weight targets for both concepts, then account for the four-station frame’s extra steel. These controls express hypothetical mass changes. They do not resize members, check capacity or predict a finished build weight.

05 / Road fit remains a hard requirement

102 inches. Every retained fitting.

The current v5 model is 103.35 inches wide including the mirrored docking-pad estimate. It fails the width requirement. The complete retained-hardware inventory and real donor dimensions are still unresolved. Neither new concept has a verified road envelope.

Reducing station count does not narrow the remaining uprights. Recessing or integrating them into the bus requires its own design; it is not assumed in these comparisons.

06 / What this enables next

Size the systems.
Then choose the lighter one.

The comparison brief is ready. A preferred design comes after the engineering and donor evidence, not from the budget sliders.

  1. Develop members and connectionsFloor options, available truss depth, transverse restraint and removable joint layouts.
  2. Establish loads and check the whole frameCrowd distribution, moving loads, wind, vibration, chassis attachment forces and vehicle stability.
  3. Reconcile with the donor when measurements are availableVerify fit, access, axle limits and all retained hardware; recalculate mass including fittings.
  4. Review before fabricationResolve the structural and vehicle-interface details, then produce member schedules and assembly drawings.
