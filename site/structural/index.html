What the results mean

For the largest conceptual crowd in this comparison, ¼-inch walls lead: 13 people. The 3/16-inch option reaches 10, and ⅛-inch reaches 7, under the declared stress criterion. A target of 20 exceeds that criterion for all three. Thicker walls alone have not resolved the 20-dancer target under these assumptions.

Even with ideal rigid shores, the ¼-inch concept reaches 14 people under this stress criterion. At the boundary counts, the transverse bearers govern. Further design work should examine those members and their connections. The ¼-inch frame and plywood add about 1,917 lb compared with ⅛ inch. At smaller crowds, a lighter option can rank better. The recommendation considers only options that clear the stress screen; when none does, it asks for a design revision.

Movement remains a separate concern. At their respective ceilings, all three calculate roughly 1.1 inches of peak deck movement under the amplified load. This study has no agreed deflection or vibration acceptance criterion, so the displayed counts do not establish comfort or dancing suitability.

The counts are lower than the earlier 20-person comparisons because iteration 03 adds an explicit criterion: twice the people load and a 207 MPa stress ceiling. Earlier iterations reported ordinary static gravity loads against assumed first yield of 345 MPa. The roof geometry and shore stiffness are unchanged.

Two shores, shown in the model

The orange vertical shores sit beneath the two edge girders, halfway between the interior bearers. Upper pads meet the girder undersides. Lower pads mark proposed structural landing zones on the Thomas; reinforcing those zones and tracing their loads to the chassis remains survey work.

The reference bus shell is shown with the framing; sheep panels, floor and equipment are hidden here for inspection. The 70 mm display struts and pad dimensions illustrate positions. They are not selected fabrication sections, and their mass remains excluded from the calculation.

2. Governing equations

Applied gravity:

fg=fD+αNWpqp(N),Wp=225 lbf

Equilibrium on free degrees of freedom:

Ktu=fg+BTr

Compression-only bearings (vertical displacement positive upward):

ri=kimax(0,−ui)

For finite k, the equivalent complementarity conditions are rᵢ ≥ 0, uᵢ + rᵢ/kᵢ ≥ 0, and rᵢ(uᵢ + rᵢ/kᵢ) = 0. The solver enumerates the four active sets of two bearings. In the rigid limit, closed bearings have uᵢ = 0 and open bearings have rᵢ = 0.

Ideal sharp-corner rectangular hollow section:

A=bh−(b−2t)(h−2t)

Iᵧ = [bh³ − (b − 2t)(h − 2t)³]/12; I𝓏 = [hb³ − (h − 2t)(b − 2t)³]/12. Member axes, section orientation and the thin-wall torsion approximation are recorded in the solver.

Section corner envelope:

σe=|Na|A+|My|h2Iy+|Mz|b2Iz

Nₐ is member axial force, distinct from the dancer count N. This envelope covers axial and biaxial bending normal stress. Values above 345 MPa flag departure from the assumed elastic range; their computed displacement does not predict post-yield movement. It does not evaluate combined shear/torsion failure, local buckling, connection capacity or a complete HSS interaction equation.

Conceptual headcount ceiling:

Nscreen=max{n∈{0,…,60}:σmax(j)≤207 MPa for every integer 0≤j≤n}

Each integer count is solved directly with its own crowd footprints. We report the last count before the first criterion exceedance. No ratio of 20-person peak stresses is used to extrapolate a headcount.

3. Load placements and maximum extraction

Each solve includes dead load, uniform full-deck loading, either full half-deck, a centered driver-side 12 × 4 ft group and an aft driver-side 12 × 4 ft group. The driver-side sweep uses 12 × 4 ft and (0.6N) × 4 ft footprints, moving in 1 ft steps plus exact centered and aftmost positions. The shorter footprint retains the prior study’s chosen crowd density. For counts above 20 the second footprint is longer. Other shapes, individual footfalls and continuous placements remain untested.

Case names of the form patch_Lft_at_Xft specify a group of length L feet starting X feet aft of the new front deck edge. The envelope includes all these cases. Stress is sampled at element ends and midpoints; deck displacement at roof nodes. Peaks of stress, drop and reaction are independent and can occur at different crowd positions. The original three-subdivision mesh is used for publication; five-subdivision replays check the boundary counts.

6. Color and score definitions

Let R = 207/σₘₐₓ at the selected headcount, and γᵧ = 345/σₘₐₓ. The displayed “yield-only factor” is γᵧ; it is not a factor of safety for the whole vehicle.

ColorRuleMeaning in this screen
GreenR ≥ 1.25 (γᵧ ≥ 2.083)At least 25% capacity-to-demand reserve above the selected criterion.
Yellow1 ≤ R < 1.25Below the stress ceiling, with less than 25% reserve.
RedR < 1Exceeds the chosen criterion.

The comparison score is S = 100[w min(R/1.25, 1) + (1 − w)Mₘᵢₙ/Mₜ], where Mₜ is this frame-and-plywood dead weight and Mₘᵢₙ is the lightest of the three. Default w = 0.70; “favor margin” uses 0.90 and “weight more importance” uses 0.50. These are explicit preference weights. Shore, connection and reinforcement weights are excluded until selected.

The recommendation selects the highest score among options satisfying R ≥ 1. If there are none, it recommends revising the design and identifies the highest-scoring option only as a comparison candidate. Scores are not reliability estimates and do not include unverified landing capacity, cost, fatigue or vehicle stability.

7. Numerical verification and reproducibility

Axial and cantilever element checks and compression-bearing opening/rigid-limit checks passed. Every solve verified applied gravity and force/moment equilibrium. For all three walls, α = 1 replay matched iteration 02 to 10⁻⁵ output units. The six default boundary cases retained their classifications with five subdivisions instead of three. Peak stress and shore reaction were unchanged at practical reported precision; the largest relative change in sampled deck drop was 0.129%. The archive includes the raw verification results.

8. What must establish an admission limit

The survey must identify and dimension actual roof bows/side posts, body mounts, front portal and rear drop-frame connections, chassis seats and engine/access clearances. A checked design must establish landing stiffness and strength, full dead loads, section properties, buckling and connection capacities, lateral stability, guardrails and stairs. Rhythmic acceleration, resonance, fatigue, braking/turning, axle loads and vehicle stability need their own assessment. No deflection or vibration acceptance criterion has been applied here.

Fabrication and operational occupancy remain unreleased. The conceptual comparison can guide the next design choice. A structural/vehicle engineer must use Earl’s measurements and the complete load cases to establish its operating limits.

References

AISC: Facts for Steel Buildings 5, Vibrations, section 5, explains rhythmic response and the distinction between vibration serviceability and strength/fatigue. AISC 360 provides the broader strength, stability and serviceability framework; this simplified screen does not claim code compliance.
