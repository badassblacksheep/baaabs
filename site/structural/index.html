Can Earl carry 12, 15 or 20 dancers?

Twenty people with ¼-inch walls stay below assumed first yield in the gravity cases examined. The driver-side girder reaches 281 MPa against an assumed 345 MPa yield stress. Peak static deck movement reaches 3.71 in. Dancing on the parked or moving bus has no established occupancy rating.

Each row uses 225 lb per person and keeps dead load in place. The rear portal carries load in this comparison. Stress and drop are independently maximized over the sampled party-side crowd positions.

Why the crowd’s position matters

With ⅛-inch walls, 15 people in the centered 12 × 4 ft patch produce 331 MPa. Moving that same footprint along the deck raises the sampled peak to 354 MPa. Allowing a shorter 9 × 4 ft group raises it to 378 MPa. Twelve people in a 7.2 × 4 ft group reach 321 MPa, about 93% of assumed yield under gravity alone.

The workshop’s 20-person, ¼-inch case produces 126 MPa and 1.67 in of drop with people spread evenly. The centered party-side patch produces 264 MPa and 3.71 in. In the placement sweep, the highest stress occurs with the group starting 14 ft from the front edge; the largest drop occurs with it starting 11 ft from the front. The peak post force and hold-down demand are 4,810 lbf and 387 lbf across this sweep.

What thicker walls buy

The modeled frame and plywood weigh 2,778 lb at ⅛ in, 3,757 lb at 3/16 in and 4,688 lb at ¼ in. Increasing all frame walls to ¼ in adds about 1,909 lb to the elevated assembly. For 20 people, 3/16 in reaches 337 MPa, about 98% of assumed yield. The ¼-inch trial reaches 81%. These percentages compare unfactored elastic normal stress to assumed material yield. A design-strength and serviceability review still has to account for load combinations, dynamic response, connections and the vehicle.

Rear portal, posts and chassis loads

The source geometry has four interior posts and a front portal. The workshop adds a mirrored rear portal with an unverified chassis load path. Each portal transfers load from its two outboard posts through a sill to two assumed inboard rail seats. Each fixed foot can resist uplift in the model. Select “Absent” to see how losing the rear support changes the results.

At 20 people with ⅛-inch walls and the rear 12 × 4 ft crowd patch, omitting the rear portal increases deck drop from 1.35 to 6.37 in and peak stress from 219 to 479 MPa. For ¼-inch walls and 20 people, the no-rear-portal sweep reaches 294 MPa and 4.02 in. A bearing-only or slipping mount would redistribute force when a seat lifts. The actual rails, plates, welds, bolts and engine clearances require measurement and design checks.

The four-post design’s 100 × 60 mm edge girders are only 60 mm deep and span 21.09 ft between interior bearers. Girder bending controls the stress comparison. In the separate archived single-post geometry, the uniform 20-person case with ⅛-inch walls reached about 929 MPa in a girder. Its lower deck and different bracing make it a distinct earlier design.

What still determines an operating rating

The analysis excludes rhythmic dancing, vehicle braking and turning, bumps, wind, fatigue, local tube-wall buckling, weld and bolt capacity, guardrails, and whole-vehicle stability. Rails, stair, art and unweighed equipment are omitted from the deck dead load. Modeled sound shelves attach to the body. Measure actual equipment weights and load paths. Agree on deflection, vibration and acceleration criteria for parked and moving use. Floor response to rhythmic loading depends on frequency and damping. AISC guidance on floor vibrations.

The next geometry comparison is a deeper longitudinal girder across the 21.09 ft interior span, evaluated against the same crowd cases, stiffness and mass targets. Survey the actual chassis interfaces and check connections, dynamic floor response, axle loads and vehicle stability with a qualified structural/vehicle engineer before establishing occupancy or issuing fabrication drawings.
