How the wings work

Each leaf pivots around its long inboard edge. A controlled lift takes it just above level so transverse support arms can slide out underneath. Pin the arms, seat the outer edge and engage the leaf’s anti-lift latch. Reverse the order to fold down. Each side gets four support lines across its three leaves, sharing the seat at each seam. Teal receivers stay under the fixed floor in all three views. At level, the arms project beneath the leaves and removable saddles bridge up to their undersides. In the folded and half-raised views the arms nest inside the receivers.

The outer edge loads those arms. Spaced receiver bearings under the fixed floor transfer the resulting shear and bending into the primary grid, then into the side beams, shores and portals. This gives the wings a bus-supported path that leaves the DJ opening clear. Receiver engagement, anchorage, clearance to the existing plan braces and the shared seat at a seam are detail-design items. Each side must carry an uneven crowd without relying on weight on the opposite wing.

The model shows the leaf frame, support arms, open-ended receivers, keeper pins, removable saddles and event railing. Hardware envelopes illustrate fit and motion. The lift, leaf latches, travel restraints and connection details remain open. One-sided loading governs a different case from an evenly filled floor. Edge crowding, rhythmic motion, guard loads and roll need to be resolved before setting an occupancy or permitting occupied movement.
