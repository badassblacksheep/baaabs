Build the floor once

The first build in the Seattle courtyard is the dance floor on Earl. I want to assemble it, inspect it and rehearse the changeover there, then take as much of that tested assembly to playa as I can. The playa-bound Wool Bus photographs show the retained framing and what appears to be decking. Its material, coverage and attachment detail are uncertain.

The floor itself changes in Rev 08. Removable plywood bears directly on transverse joists between two continuous longitudinal side beams. The steel stays on Earl between events. I’m comparing travel with the plywood fitted against travel with it stowed.

What fixing the platform buys

The candidate keeps two continuous side beams, cross joists, horizontal X-bracing and knee-braced end portals. The baseline uses five intermediate pairs between the end portals, following the owner’s count on BAAAHS. Each side therefore has five body landings plus the two portal stations. This removes regular highway-exposed field splices, duplicate truss-end uprights, lower side chords, repeated side diagonals and the lift-off cassette frames. The plywood remains independently replaceable. Flat sheets, guards, stairs, head and skin can still come off for service or transport.

“Fixed” describes the floor’s service configuration. Its interface with the flexible vehicle still needs engineered mount geometry. Resolve fore-aft and lateral restraint in both directions, including portal-to-beam joints and end-bay stability. Check buckling and eccentricity of the taller shore posts, uneven support settlement, clear height for the hatch and attachment access. Never infer permission to weld to Earl’s chassis from these photos.

How much does a missing support matter?

The screen treats one bay as a simply supported prismatic beam with uniform line load. It uses the inherited trial edge-girder section, elastic modulus and design wall. It isolates span sensitivity and omits continuity, support compliance, dynamics, post buckling, joint behavior and vehicle loads. The beam and joist sections are trial inputs inherited from the earlier study. Compare local floor bounce, beam stress, racking and connection loads before selecting stock. If a real load path needs more depth, first strengthen the governing bay or end connection and compare it with the deeper Rev 07 option. Site assembly time, mass, stiffness and service access all enter that decision.

Courtyard build order

  1. Dance floor alone. Survey the bus, fit one unloaded bay on stands and establish how the rest of the frame will stay stable during assembly. For the full parked trial, record as-built diagonals, deck level, support contact, joint positions, hatch travel and clearances before and after an engineer-specified nonhuman load test. Agree on loads, deflection, residual set, slip and stop criteria before testing. Log the result against the exact assembly. An occupied deck calls for its own review of guards, access and rhythmic loading.
  2. Add the head. Fit the existing geodesic head as a separate module after the floor trial. Develop its independent pickup on a verified primary node. The former lower-chord pickups have disappeared. Weigh it and check how its eccentric load changes portal reactions, deflection and sightlines. Keep its mounts accessible for removal. Inspect the floor again before adding the body.
  3. Add the panels. Start with a representative registered bay. Rework the panel and audio adapter interfaces for the single-level beam, including an independent reaction for each overturning couple. Check the driver-side DJ opening, exits, tool access and skin removal. Review partial-skin wind loads before building out the remaining bays. Recheck support contact and deck alignment in the complete installation.

What changes with plywood fitted?

The plywood introduces a broad surface for pressure above and below the deck. The uplift path runs from each sheet through its fixings, joists and side beams into the portal restraints. Size the independent steel bracing for the open configuration. The loss of the deep trusses, the changed joist seats and the lower plan bracing require a new global analysis. Rev 07 crowd results apply to their original member arrangement.

This sensitivity uses uniform net pressure times area. It supplies neither a highway pressure nor a fastener capacity. Determine highway relative wind, under-deck flow, edge and corner suction and asymmetric loading for Earl. The open configuration needs its own projected-member drag, vibration and fatigue assessment.

For each road version, weigh the assembled bus by axle with fuel, crew, secured cargo and the chosen plywood location. Record ride height and compare against axle, tire and gross ratings. Evaluate roll stability, braking transfer and the actual route clearances. A static crowd analysis covers a different direction and history of loading.

Seattle rehearsal and playa turnaround

  1. Survey Earl. Start at the forward driver-side shore. Trace the body and chassis interfaces from a common longitudinal datum, mark concealed segments and photograph the joints. Map each roof hatch and the side exit. Check the plywood loading route and restraint space with the intended seats installed.
  2. Rehearse one bay at ground level. Mock up a side beam, full-span joists, center-seam blocking, two adjoining plywood faces and the actual hatch envelope. Try a middle face with neighboring faces installed. Record clearance, tool access, missed-lock visibility and removal time. Choose the opening layout from the measured hatch sweep.
  3. Verify the road assembly. Agree on load cases, instrumentation and stop criteria with the reviewing engineer before any loaded or moving test. After approval for the particular test, compare baseline and post-test measurements for joint slip, loosening, permanent movement, shore contact and sheet damage. A road test needs a separate release for its location and configuration.
  4. Time a complete changeover. For the packed baseline and each retained option, start at parked arrival with the same crew and end at the same inspected event configuration. Include unloading, safe roof access, guards, stairs, fitting faces where needed, shell work and inspections. Time strike separately through final travel inspection. Record crew size, elapsed minutes, person-hours and rework for each run.

On playa, inspect the retained joints and hatch clearances first. Establish protected access for floor and guard installation, then fit stairs, shell and event equipment in the reviewed sequence. Reverse the rehearsal at strike and document the final travel configuration. If highway restraint or support performance cannot be verified, revisit the structure before committing to a fixed build.
