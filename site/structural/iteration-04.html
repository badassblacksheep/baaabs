A dancefloor. A clear cabin.

Keep the 38 × 8 ft deck and the sheep. Aim for 20 dancers. Put structure where it works, and keep the cabin free of new uprights.

Leading concept: three shore pairs, 6-inch-deep edge girders, ⅛-inch roof walls, ¼-inch front/rear portal sills, zero cabin uprights.

It clears the chosen normal-stress screen at 20 people, including 2,311 lb of hanging skins and mounting allowance. Its comparison score is 95.1 / 100.

As modeled conceptually, subject to change once we survey our specific bus, Earl Thomas. This is a design candidate, not a safe admission limit or permission to fabricate. Protecting cabin occupants requires verified load paths, body reinforcement, connections, stability and dynamic performance.

20 is still the target

Eight was the minimum threshold for considering removal of the cabin uprights. We selected the final candidate at 20, with the full panel allowance.

Shore landings are decisive

The leading model sends about 5,006 lbf into its most heavily loaded shore. A roof-skin pad is not an established load path. Each landing must engage verified structural members and transfer its load to the chassis.

Movement and failure remain open

The amplified load predicts 1.11 inches of deck drop. Softer body support, a lost shore or a larger rhythmic force can exceed the stress criterion. These are unresolved design limits.

What changed—and what helped

Depth and load-path placement beat indiscriminately thicker steel. The earlier 100 × 60 mm edge girders were only 60 mm deep vertically. We tested rotating the existing rectangles, then aligned 4- and 6-inch-deep grids. The final grid uses 6 × 2-inch edge girders and 4 × 2-inch joists; no joist interval exceeds 24 inches. Joist lines coincide with the principal support stations. Plan X bracing is retained.

We removed all four cabin uprights and their two dedicated transverse bearers from the selected concept. The front and rear corner portals remain. Shore pairs are redistributed over the front-to-rear portal span. The previously requested interior-only spacing was also tested; without cabin supports it leaves longer outer spans and generally performs worse.

The portal sills became the bottleneck. With their earlier common 3/16-inch wall, even the three-pair ⅛-inch roof calculated 208.9 MPa at 20 people with full skins, just above 207 MPa. Increasing the two portal-sill walls to ¼ inch reduced that result to 168.0 MPa. Their outer dimensions remain the modeled 110 × 130 mm. Other portal tubes retain a common assumed 3/16-inch wall.

With three pairs, all three roof-wall options reach a 25-person stress-screen ceiling with full skins. That does not mean 25 can be admitted. The sills govern before additional roof wall buys more headcount; ¼-inch roof walls add about 1,317 lb over ⅛ inch. The lighter option therefore leads this score.

1. Design space and selection rule

The first search evaluated 180 candidates at eight people with the full hanging-skin allowance: zero, two or four cabin posts; one, two or three shore pairs; interior or portal-span spacing; 4- or 6-inch edge girders; three roof walls; and, where cabin posts remained, 6 × 2 or 8 × 3-inch transverse bearers. The two-post trial retained the forward transverse pair; every possible one-pair location was not optimized.

Because zero-post candidates cleared eight, the final target-20 search retained zero cabin posts. It compared 36 cabin-free candidates after increasing the portal sills to ¼ inch. The published interactive study directly solves 27 combinations of three shore counts, three roof walls and three skin shares, for every integer headcount through the first failure and at least 30. This is a finite search, not proof of a global optimum.

Selection first honors the cabin preference, then requires the 20-person normal-stress screen, then maximizes the declared reserve/weight score. Unresolved checks cannot be scored as passed. The three-pair ⅛-inch concept ranks first among the tested cabin-free target-20 candidates, but that ranking does not release construction.

2. Geometry and support assumptions

Deck: 38 × 8 ft; x = −6.357811 to 5.224589 m, modeled roof joint plane z = 3.8604 m; edge girders y = ±1.1692 m. Front and rear portal x = −6.27 and 4.81355 m. The original four interior posts and their dedicated bearers are absent from the selected model. Member positions, orientations and sections are supplied in the model manifest and solver geometry.

For P pairs, xⱼ = xfront + j(xrear − xfront)/(P + 1), j = 1,…,P. Each x has driver and passenger shores. The competing interior-spacing study uses −3.475670 and 2.952047 m as its end datums.

HDX literature establishes architecture, not Earl’s station coordinates or capacity. Thomas describes extended roof bows and double-bolted body mounts. The reviewed drawing is a different bus order and its seat dimensions are not roof-bow spacing. Published literature therefore guides the intended body-to-chassis load path, while these exact equal-spacing coordinates remain provisional. Survey actual bows, side posts, underfloor bearers and mounts; relocate supports or design spreaders, then re-run the geometry. No bearing strength is assigned to roof sheet.

Each shore is a compression-only vertical spring with k = 5,000 lbf/in, zero initial gap and no lateral or rotational restraint. Springs are assumed independent; shared body flexibility and coupled load spreading are not modeled. The source roof height is a display datum, not a measured structural landing elevation.

Rear assumption retained: both rear corners transfer gravity through the rear chassis drop-frame into the rails, using the front portal’s modeled section scheme and fixed rail-seat restraint. Four remaining rail-seat nodes can develop moment and uplift; actual rear construction, hold-downs and engine clearances remain unverified.

3. Materials, dead load and skin allowance

E = 200 GPa, ν = 0.30, steel density 7,850 kg/m³, assumed Fᵧ = 345 MPa. Standard rectangular roof members use published ASTM A500 section area, major/minor inertia, torsional constant and stock weight from Atlas Tube. The table already reflects design-wall effects; we do not apply 0.93 twice. Metric reference members use sharp-corner hollow-section formulas with tdesign = 0.93tnominal; their gravity uses nominal-wall volume. This is more explicit than iteration 03’s effective-wall, sharp-corner comparison.

Roof walls tested: nominal ⅛, 3/16 and ¼ inch. Original posts/portals are held at nominal 3/16 inch, except the selected front/rear sills at ¼ inch. Catalog dimensions do not verify a purchased grade or batch. Local buckling, shear, torsion, axial-flexural interaction and connection limit states still require a checked design.

Plywood: 19.05 mm, 600 kg/m³, gravity only. The display slab is a schematic deck envelope. Selected panel grade, span rating, edge blocking, concentrated foot loads and fastening are not checked; a 24-inch joist limit is a layout choice, not a plywood approval.

Each new shore is estimated as a 70 × 70 mm tube with nominal ⅛-inch wall, plus a 180 × 160 × 20 mm upper plate and 600 × 180 × 20 mm lower plate. Length follows the modeled roof and landing elevations. The mass is lumped as gravity at its girder node in every load case. This is an explicit approximation; the illustrated tube and plates are not selected bearing or connection details. Additional body reinforcement and full equipment loads remain unweighed.

Skin allowance: 1,849 lb estimated bare 20-gauge perforated-steel panels × 1.25 = 2,311.25 lb. The 25% mounting addition is a planning allowance, not a measured BOM. At 50%, the roof receives 1,155.625 lb. The remaining skin load needs another load path; it does not disappear from the vehicle. This is hanging gravity only, with no credited composite/diaphragm stiffness.

4. Loads, equations and envelopes

f = fframe+floor+shores + fhung skins + αN(225 lbf)q(N), with α = 2.

Ku = f + Bᵀr; rᵢ = kᵢ max(0, −uᵢ).

Equivalently: rᵢ ≥ 0, uᵢ + rᵢ/kᵢ ≥ 0, rᵢ(uᵢ + rᵢ/kᵢ) = 0.

σ = |Naxial|/A + |Mᵧ|h/(2Iᵧ) + |M𝓏|b/(2I𝓏).

σmax ≤ 0.60Fᵧ = 207 MPa is the chosen screen.

Nscreen = max{n : σmax(j) ≤ 207 MPa for every integer 0 ≤ j ≤ n}, searched through 60.

The 3D Euler–Bernoulli frame uses six degrees of freedom per node and rigid member joints. Coincident centerline intersections are idealizations. Coping, welds, splice rotations, bearing eccentricities and the physical vertical offsets between floor, girders and joists require detailed connection modeling. These omissions are especially relevant to the new deeper sections.

Each integer headcount includes dead load, full-deck uniform, either half-deck, centered and aft driver-side groups, and longitudinal sweeps of 12 × 4 ft and (0.6N) × 4 ft crowd footprints. Sweeps step by one foot and include centered/aft extremes. Each live placement is combined with balanced, entirely driver-side and entirely passenger-side hanging skin, distributed along the edge girders. Actual discrete panel hangers, front/rear clustering, impact, wind and sway remain unmodeled.

Stress is sampled at element ends and midpoints, displacement at roof nodes. Stress, drop and shore force envelopes can come from different placements. The 2× multiplier is an equivalent-static sensitivity, not a demonstrated upper bound on rhythmic force. AISC vibration guidance requires attention to natural frequency, forcing harmonics, damping, strength and fatigue.

Loss of a shore is not tolerated by this screen. The one-shore test removes the middle driver-side spring; the pair-loss test removes both middle springs. Shore self-weight remains applied. These limited accidental scenarios do not establish robustness. Occupant protection needs verified attachments, bracing, load redistribution and an explicit abnormal-load strategy; reducing confidence in an assumed spring is not an acceptable operating procedure.

7. Colors, factors and scores

R = 207/σmax. Green: R ≥ 1.25; yellow: 1 ≤ R < 1.25; red: R < 1. The yield-only factor is 345/σmax, not a factor of safety for Earl.

S = 100[w min(R/1.25, 1) + (1 − w)Mmin/M]. Default w = 0.70; optional 0.90 or 0.50. M is frame, plywood and estimated shore weight, excluding the common skin allowance. Mmin = 3,071.25 lb across the 36 final cabin-free candidates. Scores do not represent reliability and do not include unverified body strength, vibration, connection cost or failure tolerance.

8. Verification and reproducibility

Element axial/bending checks, unilateral spring opening and rigid-limit checks passed. Every published solve asserts applied gravity, force and moment equilibrium, and bearing complementarity. Nine target-20 cases retain their classifications with five subdivisions instead of three. The solver avoids subdividing very short geometric intervals; its cached, diagonally equilibrated solution is residual-corrected and checked against the same equilibrium tolerances.
