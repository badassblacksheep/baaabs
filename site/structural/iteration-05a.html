Why favor the accessible truss? It combines the deck and potential skin supports while preserving permanent access, with no credited load path through Earl’s body. It adds about 969 lb versus 04 and puts more demand on the end connections. That is a fit to our priorities, not proof of a universal optimum.

03 / Follow the load

Can the truss be shored to Thomas?

Conceptually, yes. Shores can provide additional paths into verified body structure and onward to the chassis. Earl’s actual landing members, connections and stiffness have to establish how much help is available.

Adding shores to the exact accessible truss has not been calculated.

Side view: the shored drawing shows three of six shores. Dashed orange lines indicate assumed body-to-chassis load paths, not surveyed structural members. The 3D model above always shows the current accessible truss.

Place future shores at engineered truss nodes and verified body landings, preserving the access bays. Thin roof sheet alone is not the modeled load path. The newer HDX construction brochure shows body and chassis architecture; it supplies no added-deck capacity for our 2007 bus.

04 / Understand the limit

What does “fails the check” mean?

Keep the current truss, 20 people and 250 lb of gear fixed. Change one assumption and see what the calculation says.

Doubling dancer weight is a chosen load sensitivity, not a validated bound on dancing forces.

2× and 3× are force assumptions. They do not mean “ordinary dancing” and “jumping in unison.” Resonance, damping and actual movement have not been solved here.

Steel tube—with the wall spelled out.

The math underneath

Same calculations. Visible assumptions.

05a reads the original iteration 05 dataset. Controls select directly solved rows. They do not interpolate between crowds, extrapolate a capacity, or run a dynamic simulation.

1. Assemble the load

f=fframe+floor+fskins+fgear+αn(225 lbf)q(n)

n is the selected number of people. q(n) distributes their total force over each tested crowd footprint. α = 2 for the main controls, or 3 in the heavier sensitivity case.

4,493.35 + 2,311.25 + 250 + 2 × 20 × 225 = 16,054.60 lbf

11,555 lb physically carried in this model; the force multiplier adds no physical mass.

Dead loads remain unmultiplied. The model excludes unweighed guards, stairs, sound equipment, connection hardware and reinforcement. Skin and gear are distributed along the edge girders in balanced, all-driver-side and all-passenger-side cases; actual brackets and tote trays need local checks.

2. Solve the idealized frame

Ku=f

K contains the member stiffnesses and assumed restraints; u contains displacement and rotation. The 3D Euler–Bernoulli beam model assumes rigid member joints and fixed chassis seats in the main case. It credits no body springs in the accessible truss.

The earlier shored comparison assumes six independent compression-only paths, each k = 5,000 lbf/in. Their real stiffness and capacity are unknown. The pinned sensitivity releases foot rotation.

3. Compare the normal-stress envelope with the chosen ceiling

σe=|Na|A+|My|h2Iy+|Mz|b2Iz

Axial force Nₐ and bending moments Mᵧ, M𝓏 act on section area A and second moments Iᵧ, I𝓏. The section dimensions are b and h. The reported result is the largest sampled value across the tested placements.

σscreen=0.60Fy=0.60×345=207 MPa

Display percentage = 100 × σₘₐₓ / 207. Green: ≤80%; yellow: >80% through 100%; red: >100%. These are stress-screen colors, not probabilities of safety or a whole-vehicle factor of safety.

Currently selected governing stress member / placement: Truss vertical driver 4 · patch_12ft_at_8ft|panels_driver.

The tested patterns include full-deck, half-deck and side groups plus longitudinally swept crowd footprints. Stress, drop and support-force peaks may come from different placements. Buckling, full combined-force strength, connection/local-wall failure, fatigue and vehicle loads are outside this check.

5. What a dynamic assessment still has to solve

Mu¨+Cu˙+Ku=f(t)

This time-dependent equation is not solved in iteration 05. Mass M, damping C, natural frequencies and time-varying crowd forces matter. A “no synchronized jumping” rule cannot be derived from the 2× and 3× static-force comparisons.

AISC’s vibration guidance explains resonance and distinguishes vibration serviceability from strength and fatigue.

6. Verification, limitations and source

The underlying 05 work replays the inherited element, bearing, equilibrium and iteration-04 boundary checks. A finer beam subdivision reproduced the selected 20-person / 250 lb case at 190.03 MPa and 0.680 in. These verify the calculation implementation; they do not verify Earl’s real connections or establish occupancy.

The model keeps the 38 × 8 ft deck and existing sheep geometry. Survey the roof, portals, rear drop-frame, engine access and actual body members. Resolve material stock sizes, welds, bolts, buckling, dynamic behavior, axle loads, stability and the retained 102-inch road envelope before a reviewed fabrication release.
