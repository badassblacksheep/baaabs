Walking surfaces

Each cassette has its own steel perimeter, internal ribs and replaceable plywood. Removable carriers span across the bus under each row of cassette ends. Changing the cassette layout moves those carriers. The truss splices and panel corners stay put. Carrier seats away from truss nodes introduce local chord bending that needs a separate check.

For a single simply supported rib under the same midpoint force, deflection scales with the cube of its clear span. The narrower cassette shortens that span. Its longer perimeter rails and extra carrier contacts still need local checks. The square cassette is the first handling trial. It minimizes plywood offcuts in this comparison. The larger rectangle reduces picks and joints, and the narrow cassette trades more joints for a smaller turning envelope. Assemble each on the same reference face. Sound on the driver side

The lower cradle study reaches outward at the lower chord before dropping alongside Earl. A diagonal returns the arm force to an upper receiver. The two drops bracket the DJ window in side elevation. Body clearance, skin access and an acoustically open route from a future subwoofer still need a physical fit check. Gravity and overturning loads reach two truss elevations. The empty upper receivers reserve a path for an approved array adapter. Every cabinet family brings its own pick locations, load limits and rigging procedure. A future cradle or array needs cabinet retention, fatigue, body clearance and combined loading checks.

First listen at several roof positions with the main system at its intended level. Record coverage and conversation intelligibility. If fill is needed, trial several small speakers at low level on the passenger edge, aimed across the roof toward the party side. Permanent roof mounts can wait for that listening test.

What changes when the truss comes apart?

Three separate probes compare structures under identical small loads. A centered downward force measures vertical compliance. A roll couple measures twist. A force on the driver rail shows how one-sided loading combines the two. The body-shore springs carry compression only. Plywood, skin and equipment contribute zero global stiffness in this study.

One-sided audio, vehicle motion and stability

Axle increments use the surveyed wheelbase and the stage center in the model. The calculation assumes level ground and static equilibrium. Weigh Earl by axle and by wheel position in travel and deployed trim, including fluids, people, skins, stairs, rack, audio and hardware. Combine first moments to find the loaded center of gravity. Compare against actual axle, tire and gross ratings. Use track, suspension response, support polygon, terrain and loaded CG height for roll, braking transfer and crosswind review. Keep parked, low-speed deployed and highway-packed cases separate.

Vehicle equilibrium and missing inputs

For added masses mᵢ at measured locations rᵢ, the combined CG is (M₀r₀ + Σmᵢrᵢ)/(M₀ + Σmᵢ). On level ground, each added weight W contributes W(x − xfront)/wheelbase to the rear axle and the remainder to the front. In a rigid, level braking screen, longitudinal load transfer is Maₓh/wheelbase, with h measured above the ground. A lateral force at height h adds a roll moment Fᵧh. Include the existing CG offset, wind moment and suspension compliance before interpreting any wheel unloading. Baseline mass, CG height and track remain unmeasured.

Wind and vibration at the panel

Use net pressure for each orientation and exposure, including suction, uplift and internal flow around openings. Integrate projected areas without double-counting overlapping faces. Transfer eccentric forces through tabs, bents and primary nodes to the asymmetric vehicle reactions. Check partial skins during erection and the packed configuration separately. Test panel-edge restraint for flutter, fretting, tear-out and fatigue. Site wind criteria, gust factors and a weather operating plan need review. ESTA outdoor structure reference and scope.

Where the reactions go

The model retains the end portals, sills and three provisional shore pairs. The take-apart end posts and sill joints remain idealized in the global model. Establish their moment restraint as well as that of the longitudinal splices. End reactions must reach the chassis rails through verified seats and, aft, the drop-frame. A shore reaction needs a saddle over a verified structural bow, a continuous path down the body into the floor and mounts, and verified transfer to the chassis. The roof sheet supplies no assumed bearing capacity. Check support settlement, abrupt loss, uplift, lateral transfer and differential chassis twist with the real body connections.

The first shop bay

Build an unloaded ground-level fit-up of one bay plus a short mating return. Lay out the two side planes and one transverse seam at full size on independent stands. Start with marked stock already in the shop, plywood offcuts and replaceable dummy tabs. Record actual outside dimensions, wall, material identification and weight. Scrap can reproduce an envelope without qualifying the eventual structural section.

  1. Mark a common origin, top plane, longitudinal index and driver-side face. Make a reusable pattern for the node face. Use one fixed datum and a removable alignment aid so tolerance does not accumulate from one end.
  2. Fit a transverse tie, a plan diagonal and a mating chord stub. Dry-fit one cassette at a shared carrier. At one outer node, mock up a crown bent to an actual registered sheet outline and a separate equipment dummy. Check simultaneous wrench access, pin extraction, lifting clearance, clearance to the DJ window and removal of a middle cassette.
  3. Measure both diagonals, face squareness, hole registration, surface step and unloaded joint play. Record the kit mass, longest handling envelope, number of tools, crew and elapsed assembly/teardown time. Repeat in reverse order and with the mating part turned end-for-end where interchangeability is intended.
  4. Carry a lightweight full-envelope template from the shop through Earl’s door and turn it into the proposed rack. Record the limiting corner and clearance. Give the measurements and fit-up photos to the engineer before a separate instrumented connection test is specified.

For field assembly, first establish surveyed reaction points and independent temporary stability. Pair and brace the first two side sections, close the transverse frame and plan bracing, then add bays with the engineered erection sequence. Verify geometry and inspect every primary connection before secondary steel, cassettes, guards, stairs, panel skins or empty equipment adapters go on. Mark hardware and inspection access. Teardown needs an explicit temporary-stability sequence as restraint disappears.
