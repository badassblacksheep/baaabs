Iteration 05 · November 14, 2026 · Concept study

One structure. Room for the stuff.

A 38 × 8 ft dancefloor, the same sheep, a clear cabin—and reachable controllers, cables and little totes beneath the deck.

Advance an accessible, end-supported side-truss concept for engineering layout.

It combines the deck and potential skin mounting points, with permanent access openings and no structural credit from Earl’s roof/body. At 20 people, full skins and a provisional 250 lb service-storage allowance: 190.0 MPa and 0.68 in calculated drop.

This is a candidate, not a fabrication release or an admission limit. The front/rear chassis mounts, rear drop-frame assumption, connection strength, buckling, vibration and cabin protection are unresolved. A larger rhythmic-load sensitivity fails the chosen stress criterion.

The cabin stays clear

Zero added cabin uprights. Zero body shores in this concept. Gravity goes through side trusses and the front/rear portals to assumed rail seats. Those end connections now carry larger forces.

Access is structural

One permanent opening on each side, approximately 50.5 × 25.5 in clear in the beam model. Reinforced jambs and lower chords are included in the calculation. No loaded brace has to come out to reach a tote.

Light service storage

Lighting and sound controllers, cables and small totes. Reserve a 24-inch-wide central service strip; place shallow, secured trays within reach of removable skin panels. A 250 lb placeholder is included, not a rated storage capacity.

Inspect the candidate

Choose a directly solved case. Each includes all 2,311 lb of provisional skins and mounts. Equipment weight is tested balanced and entirely on either side, like the skin allowance.

The accessible truss adds 969 lb to the frame/floor/support estimate versus iteration 04. It reduces dependence on unverified body stiffness and preserves access. It does not win every weight or stress comparison. These figures include the changed portal members and access reinforcement; they are not the isolated benefit of adding diagonals.

Keep the decorative skin nonstructural. Design removable hangers at engineered truss nodes, with secondary retention and local load checks. Common support locations may avoid duplicate panel framing, but no such mass saving is credited here. All 206 scheduled panels and the finished sheep form remain the design requirement.

A separate skin structure is an option, not free weight removal. On iteration 04, moving all skin gravity away from the roof changes 168.0 MPa / 1.11 in to 151.3 MPa / 0.99 in. The 2,311 lb still belongs on the vehicle, along with the unweighed independent support structure. On the accessible truss, the corresponding zero-equipment results are 189.5 / 0.67 versus 184.7 / 0.59. That limited improvement does not justify assuming a second frame is automatically better.

The crawl-space access contract

  • Light controllers, cables and small totes; no batteries, subwoofers, fuel or other heavy cargo are assumed in this allowance.
  • Reachable trays near both side openings, tie-downs, cable strain relief, ventilation and removable nonstructural access panels. These details and their mass still need a layout and BOM.
  • Keep the central 24-inch service strip free of totes and cable bundles. It is reserved space, not a separately load-rated walking or crawling floor.
  • Nominal height above the raised transverse ties to the joist underside: 22.6 in. The ties clear the source model’s roof crown by about 2.0 in unloaded. Actual roof shape, tolerance and loaded clearance must be measured before adopting this geometry.
  • The deeper 0.9 m trial with straight bottom-level ties was rejected because those ties enter the modeled roof envelope. The revised 0.8 m side trusses keep the cross-ties raised above the roof.
  • Simply omitting two diagonals from the 0.6004 m trial reached 236.4 MPa at 20 people. The access opening is designed into the calculation; it is not a removable structural brace.

What is solved—and what is still open

Geometry and members

The roof joint plane stays at z = 3.8604 m, with side truss planes y = ±1.1692 m and front/rear portals x = −6.27 / 4.81355 m. The trusses span 36.36 ft between portals inside the unchanged 38 ft deck. Eight bays per side are nominally 54.5 in center-to-center. Bay 4 (zero-based), x ≈ −0.728 to +0.657 m, is open on both sides. Landing coordinates remain model datums, not a survey.

Trial sections: upper chords 6 × 2 × ⅛ in; lower chords 6 × 2 × 3/16 in; joists 4 × 2 × ⅛ in; ordinary webs/verticals and raised transverse ties 2 × 2 × ⅛ in; access jambs 4 × 2 × ⅛ in. Enlarged end-portal posts are 4 × 4 × ¼ in, with 8 × 3 × ¼ in transverse sills, 8-inch dimension vertical. Other reference portal members retain iteration 04 assumptions. The larger joints, plates, splices and chassis reinforcement are not designed or fully weighed. These are analytical inputs, not a purchase list.

The inherited 3D Euler–Bernoulli frame uses rigid member joints, E = 200 GPa and an assumed yield stress of 345 MPa. The chosen screen is 0.60Fᵧ; α = 2 is an equivalent-static sensitivity, not a validated bound on dancing. Plywood contributes gravity only. Standard rectangular HSS properties use the Atlas A500 table; other sections use sharp-corner properties and 0.93 nominal wall for stiffness. Floor, brace, weld, local-wall and global buckling checks remain separate.

Live-load patterns retain iteration 04’s full/half-deck, side-group and longitudinal swept crowd footprints. Full skin gravity is 1,849 × 1.25 = 2,311.25 lb. Skin and equipment loads are smeared along the edge girders in balanced, all-driver and all-passenger cases; concentrated hangers, tote shelves and cable trays still need explicit local checks. No strength is credited to panels. Both rear corners still use the owner-authorized assumed drop-frame transfer; the real rail seats and their fixity are unknown.

The default model contains about 7,055 lb of frame, floor, skin allowance and service storage before people. At 20 × 225 lb, that is about 11,555 lb physically carried, before unweighed guards, stairs, speakers, reinforcement and hardware. The 2× crowd calculation applies a larger equivalent force; it is not additional physical mass. Axle loads, center of gravity, vehicle stability, road configuration and the 102-inch retained-width requirement remain open.

Pinned feet change the calculated maximum normal stress only slightly but increase rail-seat force and introduce uplift. That is a changed load path, not evidence of safer attachments. The 3× live-load trial exceeds the criterion. No claim of abnormal-load resilience, dynamic acceptance or protection of cabin occupants follows from a passing normal-stress row.

Design decision

Carry this accessible shared-truss concept into the survey and connection layout. Keep the body-shored grid as a lighter comparison. The preferred direction is to make the engineered frame and verified chassis interfaces carry the dancefloor and skin, while preserving removable access and light service storage. If the end mounts cannot accept the demands, evaluate intermediate external chassis load paths before sacrificing the cabin. Such paths must accommodate chassis twist and the removable event/road configuration; none is assumed in these numbers.

Verification and source

Iteration 04’s element checks, compression-only bearings, nine refinement comparisons, six ceiling-boundary checks and all eight report/model tests were replayed successfully. The new target case was repeated at five subdivisions instead of three: its stress classification and displacement remain consistent. Every solve checks force/moment balance; zero credited body reactions and unchanged panel-node data are tested. These are software and model checks, not engineering certification.
