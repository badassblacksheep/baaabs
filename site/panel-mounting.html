What moves during removal?

The two sheets, shaped backing and rear frame move together. The orange outriggers and seats stay on the stations. The exploded view separates the retaining hardware, then shows the region lifted above its seats and pulled outward.

Release access and clearance past neighboring regions still need to be designed. This is an explanation of the connection, not an approved removal sequence.

Follow the load

Panel → backing frame → lower seats and retaining bolts → braced outriggers → stations → chassis attachments

  1. Existing stationsBlue uprights stay at their current positions. Proposed doubler pads connect the outriggers to their outer faces.
  2. Braced outriggersOrange arms bridge the space between bus and backing frame. Lower diagonal braces make a triangular support.
  3. Two lower seatsThe region rests on ledges during installation. Its weight bears on the seats instead of depending solely on bolt friction.
  4. Four retained connectionsTwo illustrative bolts at each flange retain the assembly against outward and upward movement. Upper connections restrain rotation.
  5. Removable backing frameTeal tubes and attachment tabs carry the two existing panels together. This is a proposed grouping, not a new panel split.
  6. Isolation at interfacesPink pads indicate separation of steel and aluminum. Sleeves, drainage and movement allowance still need detailing.

Design limits

Panels B-D-033 and B-D-034 retain their current shape and position. Their skin lies approximately 0.84–1.25 m outside the upright centerline; the arms reach the nearer rear frame, with smaller members reaching the facets.

No member, weld, bolt or station connection is load-rated. Speaker loads stay on separate supports. Chassis twist, wind, road loads, thermal movement, panel pull-through and access remain to be resolved.

What the detail does and does not specify

The model uses illustrative 10 mm bolts and actual holes in the mounting flanges. Those dimensions are visual placeholders, not a hardware selection. Station pads show a possible welded interface; weld design and local upright-wall capacity are unverified. Panel screws are examples, not a fastening schedule. The lower seats and restrained upper connections express the intended load path.
