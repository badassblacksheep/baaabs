What moves during removal?

There are two separable assemblies. First, the two sheets, shaped backing and rear frame detach together. Then the two lower triangular brackets and two upper arms unbolt from the stations. Each lower bracket carries its seat and outer retainer; none of those projecting pieces remain in the Arms removed view. Compact station pads, welded-in sleeves and captive nut cages remain.

Support each assembly before releasing its bolts. Tool access and clearance past neighboring regions still need design; the exploded view is not an approved removal sequence.

Follow the load

Panel → backing frame → lower seats and retaining bolts → braced outriggers → stations → chassis attachments

  1. Existing stationsBlue uprights stay at their current positions. Compact front/back pads, internal crush sleeves and captive nuts form prepared docking points. The station bolts can be released from outside without a rear holding wrench. The sleeves are intended to prevent bolt tightening from squeezing the hollow tube walls; their design still needs checking.
  2. Braced outriggersOrange arms bridge the space between bus and backing frame. Root flanges bolt onto the stations. Each lower arm, diagonal brace, seat and outer receiver is one removable bracket; each upper arm is a separate removable tie.
  3. Two lower seatsThe region rests on ledges during installation. Those ledges leave with the lower brackets when the station bolts are removed.
  4. Four retained connectionsTwo illustrative bolts at each outer flange retain the panel frame. Separate through-bolts at the upper arm, lower arm and brace foot release the projecting steel from the station.
  5. Removable backing frameTeal tubes and attachment tabs carry the two existing panels together. This is a proposed grouping, not a new panel split.
  6. Isolation at interfacesPink pads indicate separation of steel and aluminum. Sleeves, drainage and movement allowance still need detailing.

Transport width still needs work

The current paired station uprights span 105.4 in before pads. Mirroring these docking pads on both sides would bring that to approximately 105.8 in.

Washington’s standard outside-width limit is 102 in. Removing these arms alone therefore does not establish a road configuration. The uprights will also need a transport solution, or a route/permit assessment.

The Arms removed model is a local bay only; it does not assess the head, deck, equipment, height or whole-vehicle clearance. Washington outside-width rule.

Design limits

Panels B-D-033 and B-D-034 retain their current shape and position. Their skin lies approximately 0.84–1.25 m outside the upright centerline; the arms reach the nearer rear frame, with smaller members reaching the facets.

No member, weld, bolt or station connection is load-rated. Speaker loads stay on separate supports. Chassis twist, wind, road loads, thermal movement, panel pull-through and access remain to be resolved.

What the detail does and does not specify

The model uses illustrative 10 mm panel-frame bolts and 12 mm station through-bolts, with holes and internal sleeves. These are visual placeholders, not selected hardware. The pads, sleeves and captive nut cages are shop-prepared stationary parts; the projecting arm flanges unbolt. Sleeve welds, hole weakening, bolt bearing and local upright-wall capacity are unverified. Panel screws are examples, not a fastening schedule. The lower seats and restrained upper connections express the intended load path.

Reference principles

Hollow tube through-bolts need local wall checks; internal sleeves can address wall distortion. Steel Tube Institute through-bolt guidance.
